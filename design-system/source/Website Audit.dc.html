A — The missing online store

You flagged this directly: the old site had a store and the new one has quietly lost it as a destination. It isn't gone — themonkeyvault.com/s/order is still linked in a few spots — but it's no longer a place a visitor can decide to go. Worth separating two verbs the site currently blurs: book a time (Square appointments) vs buy a pass, pack, membership or gift (Square store). They're different Square surfaces and different intents.

✓ ResolvedThe store isn't in the navigation anywhere

The old site carried a top-level “Online Store” menu with two entries — Passes & Memberships and Gift card. On the new site the header is Train / Camps / Events / Pricing / About — no store. The only ways to reach it are the footer's “Online store” link and a handful of in-body buttons (Train's “Get a day pass”, Athletic's “Join”, Pricing's gift-card band). Someone who arrives thinking “I just want to buy a 10-pack” has no obvious front door.

Done → the site now has its own Store.dc.html — “Store” is a header nav item on every page, the footer's store link is internal, and Book Now's “Passes & Memberships” group points into it. Each item card's Buy button reads its own Square checkout URL from site-config storeItems (paste per-item URLs there; empty falls back to the Square store page for now).

✓ ResolvedPricing promises a checkout it doesn't offer

The Pricing hero says the list “checks out securely in our online store” and to “set up your pass before you get to the gym.” But the live price list is display-only — it has no buy buttons — and the only store link on the whole page is the gift-card band at the very bottom. This is the exact page where purchase intent peaks, and there's nothing to click. It's also the most natural home for the store.

Done → the hero now carries a “Buy passes & memberships” CTA (with “Book a session instead” beside it), and a store/booking CtaBand sits right after the live list — both route to the site's own Store page, whose Buy buttons hand off to Square checkout.

✓ ResolvedGift-card links go to the general store, and can't be repointed

Every “Gift cards” link (footer, Pricing band) points at /s/order — the general store — whereas the old site used a dedicated gift URL (squareup.com/gift/…) that lands straight on gift-card purchase. Worse, in site-config.js the LINK_MAP maps /s/order → store only, so the config's separate giftCard key can never actually swap those links — they collide on the same URL.

Done → giftCard now holds the dedicated gift URL (squareup.com/gift/…) and has its own token in LINK_MAP; the gift-card anchors in SiteFooter / Pricing carry that URL, so they no longer collide with the store link and stay repointable from config.

E — Through the eyes of a parent & a party planner

You named these two as the audiences that matter most. Measured against how they actually choose — and against common practice for gym and kids'-venue sites — the site does a lot right (the birthday FAQ answers exactly what parents ask; “weekends fill fast” creates the right urgency). These are the gaps worth closing.

Findability (Med). A parent scanning fast should reach the schedule, pricing and “how to book” in seconds. Pricing and About are in the nav, but the schedule and the store aren't — the two most transactional pages are the two you can't navigate to. Ties directly to A1 and the header.

Social proof (Med). Parents lean on reviews and scan them for “clean, safe, friendly, organized.” The site's reviews are three unnamed sample quotes plus a “4.9★ / 300+ reviews” line that reads as a live figure but is hard-coded. Wire real Google reviews (with names), or mark the section as illustrative until they're in — right now it's the least trustworthy part of an otherwise credible site.

Real imagery (Med). A venue site sells the feeling of the room in its first seconds. Seven placeholder photo/video slots plus the GIF ribbons (D2) undercut that, and the Events “Full Party” card leans on a hot-linked Square-CDN image. Party parents in particular want to see real kids having a real party in your space.

The party “effort factor” (Med). When parents pick a party venue, the quiet question is “what do I have to do on the day?” The winning answer is stress-free / all-inclusive / a named host who runs it. Events implies this (“coaches stay with your group the whole time”) and its FAQ nails food, cake and cancellations — but it never says stress-free, we handle setup & cleanup, or “your coach hosts the whole party” up top, where it reassures fastest. A few lines of copy, high payoff.

Safety, where the parents are (Low). Safety is a must-see reassurance for parents (a threshold, not a differentiator). It's covered well on Get Started and About — but absent from Events, the page selling to party parents. Surface padded floors / foam pit / trained-coach supervision there. (The Home hero also ships an unused trust array — trained coaches, padded floors, ages 3+ — that no longer renders; those are exactly the signals worth showing.)

2 — Presenting dense information

This is the site's actual strength, worth naming so it keeps happening: three patterns already do the heavy lifting of making a lot of content feel light.

Flip cards (the Class Card, Home's three offering cards, Camps) keep the front to a scan — one photo, one line, a price — and put the rest one tap away. That's the right call on a site that has nine class types, three camp formats and five booking categories to explain without ever feeling like a wall of text.

Accordions on the FAQ and the Events FAQ do the same thing for text: eight or more questions collapse to one line each instead of a long scroll of paragraphs.

Jump Nav's scroll-spy and quick-links give the longest pages (Train, Athletic Program, Events, Book Now) a way to skip straight to a section instead of scrolling blind — and it's genuinely one shared component everywhere, not four different attempts at a sub-nav.

✓ ResolvedOne place this breaks down: Train's "Core Programs"

Recreational (drop in, no booking), Private Lessons (1-on-1, by appointment, with its own three-tier page underneath it) and the Athletic Program (a monthly membership with a prerequisite) sit side by side as three equal-weight cards under one heading. Each is a genuinely different commitment and booking model, but you can't tell which is which without opening every card.

Done → every Class Card front now carries a booking tag (Drop-in / By appointment / Membership), so the commitment model reads before you tap.
