The Monkey Vault · Design Language

The Card System

Every "pick one of these" moment on the site is one of three card types, chosen by what question the card is answering — never by habit or by what looked good on the page next to it. This is the logic, not just the look, so a new card is a decision, not a guess.

00 — The logic

Three questions, three cards

Before building a card, ask what job it's doing. That answer picks the component — it isn't a style choice.

SellClassCard

"Which of these several similar things is right for me?" The visitor is still comparing. Needs a photo, room to flip for detail, and every fact that helps them pick — including how they'd book it.

Train — Core Programs, Specialized Classes, Partnership Classes
BookBookingCard

"I already know what I want — let me book it." The decision is made; a photo would just slow the tap. Needs the price and the action up front, still with pills and a booking-method tag.

Book Now — every group, every item
IntroSimple flip tile

"What kinds of things does this place even offer?" Introducing a whole category, not one bookable item — there's no single booking model to name yet, so pills and the booking-method tag don't apply. One price line is enough; the click leads to a page that answers the real Sell/Book question.

Home's three offering tiles · Camps' three camp tiles · Events' two birthday tiles
The test

Ask: does this card need to explain how you book it? If yes and there are several to compare → ClassCard. If yes and the visitor's already decided → BookingCard. If the card is really just a doorway to a page that will answer that question → Intro tile. Never invent a fourth shape for a card that's really one of these three.

02 — ClassCard · the back, in detail

Tap the card to flip it

The front is for scanning — the back is for deciding. A one-line hook, 2–3 phrases of context, and the facts that close the decision. Same frame, same category colour, no image.

1
Eyebrow + flip-back

Class & category in the category colour, left; a quiet "Flip back" cue, right, so the way home is always visible.

2
Headline — a hook, not the title

One short promise that sells the feeling. "At your own pace, zero pressure" — never just repeat "Recreational".

3
The context — 2–3 phrases

The "why you'd pick this" that won't fit on the front. If it runs longer, it belongs on the class's own page.

4
Key facts — a labelled spec sheet ★

Label : value, not prose — locked labels, fixed order: Level → Ages → Schedule → Booking → Price. Skip a row that has nothing real to say; never rename one. Full vocabulary below.

5
The same action

Same words, same category-coloured arrow — never send the two faces to different places.

Do
  • Keep it to one screen. Hook + 2–3 phrases + 3–5 facts.
  • Lead with a benefit. The headline is a promise, not the class name.
  • End where the front ends. Same CTA, same destination.
Don't
  • Don't restate the front. No repeated title, description or pill text.
  • Don't say a thing twice. "All levels · beginner-friendly" is just "All levels".
  • Don't hide the way back. The "Flip back" cue stays in view.
  • Don't add an image. The back is words only.
Back-facts vocabulary — locked labels, fixed order ★

The back answers the questions of someone who passed the front's curiosity test, in the order they ask them. Each row is one label + one value, with at most one "·" detail. Skip rows with nothing real to say; never invent or rename a label.

Level "Can I actually do this?" All levels · Levels 1–2 · Prerequisite required. "Beginner-friendly" and "welcome" are retired — they restate "All levels".
Ages "Is it for me — or my kid?" The locked bands from section 04, expanded when useful: "All ages — Parent & Child (3+) to Adults". The old Who label is retired — same fact, one name.
Schedule "When does it run?" Only when there's a clean, memorable slot ("Tue · Thu — 7 PM") or a fixed rhythm ("2 × 90 min / week"). Messy timetables defer to the live schedule — skip the row. The old Format label folds in here.
Booking "How do I get in?" One locked phrase per method — Drop-in · no reservation needed / By appointment · reserve online / Membership · join any time / Direct with the coach. Expands the front's booking-method tag; never contradicts it.
Price "What does it cost?" The number the front deliberately never shows: single rate first, then the pack or monthly ("$30 single · 10-pack $275"). Always last — it's the fact people check after everything else fits.
Retired "Open gym included" — universal to every option, so it says nothing on any single card (same rule as pills). Who / Format / Start here — folded into Ages, Schedule and Price. One product-specific extra row is allowed only when it's the product's defining fact (Martial Arts' Disciplines).
03 — The pill rules

How to write a good pill

Do
  • 1–3 words. "Teens & adults", "Mon · Wed · Fri".
  • 2–3 pills per card. Pick only the facts that drive a decision.
  • Cover the essentials: who it's for · level · when or format.
Don't
  • No sentences. "Great for all skill levels" → "All levels".
  • No more than four. A wall of pills hides the important ones.
  • Don't repeat the booking-method tag — see section 05.
04 — Pill vocabulary

A shared set of pills

Build every card from this list so the wording stays consistent across the whole site.

Ages
Parent & Child (3–6) Kids (7–12) Teens (13–17) Adults (18+) Teens & Adults (13+) All ages

Locked: the live schedule's own filters are exactly Parent & Child / Kids / Teens / Adults. Numbers restored in parens where sourced — Private Lessons defines the boundary explicitly ("Kids ≤17 & adults 18+"); Parent & Child is ages 3–6, Kids 7–12, Teens 13–17, Adults 18+. "Teens & Adults" is the system's real combined-class label, meaning 13+.

Program-specific ages — products that diverge from the bands by design
Ages 9+ Ages 5+ Ages 6–16 Ages 8+

Some products set their own ages instead of using the bands — that's by design, and each value is only used on its own card: Athletic 9+ on the card (the 9–14 / 15+ group split is detail for its own page) · Camps 5+ generally, 6–16 for Summer Camps · Full Birthday 5+ · Mini Birthday 8+.

Format
1-on-1 Your own group

Locked: only the formats that break the norm get a pill: Private is 1-on-1, Private Day Camp is your own group with your own instructor. Dropped as givens: "Coach-led" (a class having a coach is assumed) and "Open to the public" (everything is public unless stated otherwise — the exception is private, not public).

Level
All levels Levels 1–2

"Beginner-friendly" dropped — it said the same thing as "All levels". One wording, everywhere.

Days / time
Mon · Wed · Fri — 6 PM Saturdays

Use only when a class runs the same time on its days (a clean, memorable slot). Anything more complicated defers to the live Schedule page — don't compress a messy timetable into a pill.

Group size
Min 5 kids 3–15 people Min 10 people

Minimums/ranges for group-booked programs: Private Day Camp min 5, Mini Birthday 3–15, Group events min 10. Irrelevant to per-person classes — most cards never use it.

Duration
90 min 2 hrs total

Standard classes are 60 min (per the schedule) — that default never gets a pill. Only the exceptions do: Athletic/Strength 90 min, Birthday/Group events 2 hrs total.

Access
Private room All-day pass

Locked: a standout inclusion beyond the core session — Full Birthday's private party room, Mini Birthday's all-day pass per kid. Athletic's complementary classes / evaluations were considered and dropped as pill material. Open Gym never earns a pill (it's universal — every option includes it).

Retired

Price — removed as a pill category; price already has its own dedicated slot on the cards. Booking — moved to the dedicated Booking-method tag (section 05); never re-add either as a pill.

05 — The booking-method tag

See this tag, know how to book

Decided — solid chip

One consistent signal, reused everywhere a card sits next to others with a genuinely different commitment model. Drawn deliberately differently from the pills, so it reads as its own thing rather than one more piece of meta text.

Considered, not used — on the photo, top-right (kept for reference)
Drop-in

Paired with the category tag in the very first glance, before the visitor reaches any text. Open risk: against a bright or high-motion GIF/video background, a translucent badge can lose contrast and get skimmed past — the exact "might get ignored" concern raised on this direction. Redesigned to a near-solid backdrop + thin light border (was semi-transparent) so it holds up regardless of what's behind it; still watch this on the real hero GIFs once they're swapped for video.

Final — solid chip in the content area
Membership Drop-in By appointment Register

Sits above the title, solid fill. Colour comes from the product's category (var(--cat)) — never a fixed palette per booking-method. That's deliberate: Classes are booked either drop-in or by membership, Open Gym is always drop-in, Private is always by appointment — the method varies by product, so the tag just inherits whatever colour that card already is. One rule, no exceptions, nothing new to maintain in categories.js. Reads instantly and needs no photo — the version BookingCard uses, since it has no image to place a tag on. Both are wired into ClassCard today via booking-tag-style (onImage / solid) for live A/B comparison on Train — see Booking Tag - Options.dc.html.

Use it when
  • Cards sitting next to each other book in genuinely different ways (Train's Core Programs).
  • A visitor could reasonably assume the wrong commitment level without it.
Skip it when
  • Every card on the page books the same way (it'd be four identical tags — no signal, just noise).
  • The card is an Intro tile — there's no single item's booking model to name yet.
06 — BookingCard · the decision already made

Everything Book Now is built from

A ClassCard with the decision-making already done: no photo, no flip, one face. It inherits ClassCard's category-colour, pill and booking-tag logic exactly — so the two families can never drift apart — and adds the one thing Book Now needs that Train doesn't: price, up front, next to the action.

Anatomy — same order as ClassCard, minus the photo
Accent bar Duration + booking-tag chip Optional flag Title + description Tinted pills Price + note Action
Rules of thumb
The whole card is a link — never add a second nested CTA.
Price is the one thing ClassCard's front never shows and BookingCard always does.
Add the booking-tag only when the group mixes commitment types (see section 05).
07 — Intro tiles · a doorway, not a decision

Home, Camps and Events' simpler flip tile

Three places on the site flip a tile that looks close to a ClassCard but deliberately carries less: Home's three offering tiles ("Train" / "Camps" / "Events"), Camps' three camp-format tiles, and Events' two birthday tiles. Each is introducing a category, not selling a single bookable item — so there's no one booking model to name, and pills would have nothing real to say.

What it keeps from ClassCard
  • Photo + category colour + flip-to-detail back — same physical shape and motion.
  • One price line on the front, when there's a genuine "starting from" figure (camps, birthdays).
What it deliberately drops
  • Pills — "who / level / when" doesn't exist yet at the category level.
  • Booking-method tag — the tile isn't the booking decision, its destination page is.

If this ever needs pills or a booking-method tag — e.g. a future tile starts naming a specific bookable thing instead of a category — that's the signal to promote it to a real ClassCard or BookingCard, not to grow its own copy of either feature.

08 — Category colours

One colour per category

Colour is grouped by price-list category, not by discipline — every specialized class (Parkour, Flips, Strength, Tricking, Gymnastics…) is booked as one "Classes" product, so they all share the same blue. Shared with ClassCard, BookingCard and every Intro tile — the single source of truth is categories.js. Change a colour there once.
